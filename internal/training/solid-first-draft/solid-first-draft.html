What Belongs In There

Not your life story. The things you would otherwise retype every single thread.

How You Want To Be Talked To

  • Close with a summary
  • Keep it brief
  • Use a professional, undramatic voice
  • Format — tables vs lists

Where You Are Coming From

  • I have a technical background
  • I am a manager of 20 years
  • I am generally pushing the boundary of new and innovative tech

How You Work

  • Tell me what you understand
  • Ask me about things where I am unclear
  • Ground work in sources that are validated

Your Turn — Write Three Lines (+20)

Open your profile. Add three lines. Do not overthink them — you will change them all week.

Pick From

  • One line about who you are
  • One line about how you want answers
  • One line about a habit you want it to have

Then

  • Re-run a question you asked last week
  • Notice what changed
  • Notice what did not

Watch Out

  • Long profiles get ignored
  • Contradictions get ignored
  • Be specific or skip it

Three lines. Five minutes. Raise your hand when it's saved.

Talking points

The Pro-Tips on the next two slides are not a lecture list — every one of them is a move

actually made in this thread. Walk the thread live and point to where each one happens:

opening neutral, asking it to challenge you, demanding exact quotes, asking it clarifying

questions before it answers, auditing its own work, and cross-checking it with a second tool.

The habit is demonstrated here, even though the slide itself can't show the doing.

Pro-Tips

Some habits I try to use

Make it prove it read your content

Open neutral as possible

Use the tool as your research partner

Find trusted content

Draft when you have a solid set of content

BBursera Consultingburseraconsulting.comConfidential · © 202615 / 44

Pro-Tips — More From This Thread

Make It Ask You First

Before a scoped or technical answer, tell it to ask clarifying questions rather than guess your intent

Demand Exact Quotes, Not Paraphrase

"Give me exact quotes" turns a vague synthesis into something you can check against the source

Have It Audit Its Own Work

Before you rely on a draft, ask it to re-verify its own claims against the source material

Cross-Check With a Second Tool

Don't let one model grade its own homework — bring the output to a different tool and ask it to find the holes

Notice When It Asserts What It Can't Know

Watch for confident answers about things the tool has no way to actually verify — that's a tell, not just a thread-rot symptom

Watch for Thread Rot

BBursera Consultingburseraconsulting.comConfidential · © 202617 / 44

Like a meeting with real Interns... or is it?

"It's been 2 hours I need a break"

What they really need is to tag team in a fresh set of workers. A break can make things worse.

"We talked about that over an hour ago"

Was someone taking notes?

"How was I supposed to know that was important"

What are you going to hand to the next set of workers?

What Triggers Compaction

PlatformOfficial behaviorWhat users report
ClaudeAuto-summarizes as the window fills; designed so full history stays referenceable with warning at compactionNot widely reported as a failure — matches documented design
GeminiFree: rolling FIFO, oldest turns drop. Paid: 1M window meant to avoid mid-session compactionActive complaints of context loss even on paid tiers — support threads report retention "broken" in the newest model
ChatGPTOldest content silently drops once the window fills — no summarization stepConsistent with official behavior — the "forgetting" is the documented mechanism, not a bug

Documented behavior and user experience are not always the same.

The Four Symptoms

The window is finite. Some tools will signal that the thread is 'compacting' or 'compressing'.

You have to repeat instructions

You had some idea figured out with the chat and now you are having to explain it all over again

It is making less sense not more

If the thread was tracking well and providing useful insights and now it is giving you nonsense or the opposite from prior

Drafts degrade

If you are drafting and it is dropping content

Thread Lock up

This used to be common. It would spin endlessly, or just not return, or otherwise become unresponsive

Normally after 20 or 30 turns — especially if you pulled in a lot of content or images to parse

What To Do About It

You do not fix a rotted thread. You leave it.

Draft... and Save copies

Keep the Good References

Then Start Fresh

It is going to degrade more

Watch how many turns you are in. Be mindful of how much you are loading in. Watch for quality degradation.

Finding Who To Name

You do not need to already know the field. You need to make the tool tell you.

Ask It

  • "Who are the serious voices here"
  • "Who disagrees with them"
  • "Who is popular but not respected"

Check Them

  • Ask for details about what they think
  • Ask for quotes or links
  • Did the tool invent one

Then Use Them

  • "Weigh X's position heavily"
  • "Where would Y object"
  • "What would they both miss"

Your Turn — Find Some Voices Other Than Your Own (+45)

Ask your tool who the serious voices are on your topic. Pick three. Verify they exist.

Do

  • Get a list of five to ten
  • Look up three of them
  • Add the surviving names to your prompt

Notice

  • Did it invent anyone
  • Did it name only Americans
  • Did it name only living people

Then

  • Re-run and compare sources

Push for details and examples so you know they are real.

Demonstration — Skeleton First (+50)

Figure out the Shape First

Not "write me a report." A section list, in order, with what each section is for.

The tool will happily choose the shape for you. Its shape is the average of everything it has read.

BBursera Consultingburseraconsulting.comConfidential · © 202629 / 44

The Parts of a Design

Six lines is usually enough. Write them before you write anything else.

What It Is For

  • Who reads it
  • What they decide

What Is In It

  • Sections, in order
  • What each one answers

What Is Not

  • Out of scope, explicitly
  • Where you expect thin evidence
BBursera Consultingburseraconsulting.comConfidential · © 202630 / 44

Your Turn — Sketch Yours (+55)

Write the skeleton for the paper you are building across these five sessions.

Write

  • One line: who this is for
  • Four to six section names
  • One line each: what it answers

Then Hand It Over

  • "Here is my structure"
  • "What is missing"
  • "What is in the wrong order"

Do Not Yet

  • Do not ask it to write the whole thing
  • One section is enough today
