Bibliography — the whole paper, one list, grouped by source

RES-1188 ruled: one bibliography for the whole paper, grouped by source, not by chapter. This document is that consolidation. It absorbs the existing 37-entry Generative-lane list (AIJM-S12_BIBLIOGRAPHY-GENERATIVE_v0.1.md, entries F1–F7, C1–C6, B1–B5, A1–A4, E1, G1–G3, D1–D2, N1–N9 — that document's own numbering is kept as a cross-reference key below rather than re-verified) and adds every source named in prose across the other eight live sections: Intro (v0.20), Field-history (v0.7), How-these-systems-work (v0.7), Method-Predictive (v0.18), Method-Agentic (v0.11), Terminology (v0.12), Appendix (v0.9) and Conclusion (v0.18).

Constraint honored throughout. Every entry below comes from a document already on disk in this project — a case dossier, a source-pull file, a research dossier, a verification pass, or the generative bibliography itself. Nowhere below is a URL or date supplied from this assistant's own knowledge of the source. Where the project's own record holds no link, the entry says so, in bold, and that is reported as a finding rather than completed. Two sentences in the live paper — the Sculley NeurIPS quotation and one unattributed Reiter quotation carried since the trends-brief era — have no locatable source anywhere in the project's record at all, not even a thin one; both are flagged under "Passages with no usable citation" at the end, because that absence is more serious than a thin entry.

Two decisions, made before building the list

(a) Short-form in-text reference format

Decision: (Source, short tag, Year) — the publishing organization or named author, a two-to- five-word tag distinguishing that document from others by the same source, and the year. Examples: (Bloomberg, LQA launch release, 2016) · (Bloomberg, BAI white paper, 2020) · (Reiter, "still not trustworthy", 2024) · (Chua, Semafor column, 2025) · (Sculley et al., NeurIPS 2015) · (SEC, BVAL order, 2023). Where a source has more than one document from the same source and year, add a letter: (Reiter, 2026a) / (Reiter, 2026b), keyed to the numbered entry below.

Why this form and not another. The paper's own prose already names people and organizations in-line ("John Micklethwait, Bloomberg, December 2024"), so a numbered footnote scheme would duplicate work the prose already does and would force renumbering on every edit. A full author-year form (APA-style) fails here because a third of the record's entries have no named author — a press release, a product page, a corporate blog post — and "Bloomberg, n.d." repeated sixty times is less useful than a short descriptive tag a reader can match to the entry by eye. The tag form also matches the register distinction this whole exercise turned on: (Bloomberg, ...) and (Press Gazette, reporting Bloomberg, ...) read differently on the page, which is the point — collapsing them into one undifferentiated author-year form would erase exactly the distinction RES-1188 asked this document to preserve. This is a recommendation for the format only; per scope, no in-text marker is added to the body prose in this pass.

(b) Register vocabulary — reconciled from 11 down to 9

The generative bibliography's own closing note states the authoritative six-category scheme this project declared for its case records: first-party Bloomberg, peer-reviewed, trade press, mainstream press, aggregator-relayed, sponsored content (AIJM-S12_BIBLIOGRAPHY-GENERATIVE_v0.1.md, opening paragraph and closing register-mix note). That same document then needed five more to cover sources outside the case records: practitioner expert blog, preprint (not peer-reviewed), research-institute report, conference paper held without a locator, and user-generated — eleven in total, "not a project source" recorded twelfth as an exclusion flag rather than a register.

Pulling in the Predictive and Agentic lanes' own dossiers, plus the newly reviewed Field-history, Terminology, Appendix and Conclusion prose, adds two categories that recur too often and carry too different an evidentiary weight to fold into any of the six: a U.S. government regulatory action (the SEC's BVAL order) and preprints that are never going to become peer-reviewed within this paper's window (BloombergGPT, the Hagar/Diakopoulos/Gilbert paper, several GHG-adjacent arXiv postings). This document's reconciled set is nine registers, collapsed and extended as follows:

  1. First-party Bloomberg — unchanged from the original six.
  2. Peer-reviewed — unchanged, and now also absorbs the generative list's "conference paper held without a locator" (F5, Sculley) — it is peer-reviewed (NeurIPS 2015) regardless of whether this project holds its locator; "held without a locator" was describing this project's access, not the source's own status, so it does not need to be its own register.
  3. Preprint / working paper, not peer-reviewed — kept as its own category rather than folded into peer-reviewed, because the generative bibliography's own BloombergGPT entry (G1) is emphatic that this distinction is load-bearing for how the paper cites it.
  4. Practitioner expert blog / independent commentary — kept as its own category. It recurs across every lane that touches Reiter, Harrell, Karpathy or Chua's personal-voice pieces, and folding it into trade press (as the six-category scheme would require) would misstate the authorship — these are the source's own first-person statement, not a reporter's account of one.
  5. Trade press — unchanged, and now absorbs the generative list's "research-institute report" (F7, the Tow Center report cited inside Graefe 2016) — a nonprofit research-institute report about the trade functions evidentially the same way a trade-press piece does here: independent of Bloomberg, written for an industry audience, not peer-reviewed.
  6. Mainstream press — unchanged.
  7. Aggregator-relayed — unchanged, and now absorbs the generative list's "user-generated" category (the single Apple App Store review, A5) — both are third-party content produced for a purpose other than reporting on the source, and both reach this study at one or more removes from anyone who could be asked a follow-up question. Collapsing them loses only the distinction between an anonymous relay and an anonymous review, which this project's own record does not otherwise use anywhere else.
  8. Regulatory — new. The SEC's BVAL order is categorically different from a trade-press account of that order (S06_PULL-IBVAL S6, S7): it is the primary enforcement document itself, carries legal weight none of the other eight registers share, and appears only in the Predictive lane and the Conclusion.
  9. Sponsored content — unchanged (a genuine register in the LQA and GHG pulls' Risk.net awards entries, even though the generative list's own case record used it zero times).

"Not a project source" stays outside the register system entirely, as an exclusion flag rather than a category — a document identified as contamination (aicerts.ai, G3) is not weighed as evidence at all, so giving it a register would imply it belongs in this list on the same footing as the other 90-plus entries. It stays here only because the paper names and rebuts it.
